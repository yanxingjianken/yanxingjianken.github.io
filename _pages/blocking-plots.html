Atmospheric blocking is a large, persistent high-pressure system that stalls the usual west-to-east progression of weather for days to weeks, often bringing heat waves, cold spells or drought. These pages show how blocks form and decay, and what the weather pattern looks like now.

Contents. Two viewers, a daily prediction check and a model. The first viewer shows composite fields of Northern Hemisphere blocking and propagating anticyclones from the PV-tendency analysis (ERA5). The second shows the latest operational forecast over the Northern Hemisphere at 850, 500 and 250 hPa, updated every 6 h, with 500-hPa cyclone and anticyclone centres tracked over the preceding five days. The third item checks every day whether Rossby wave breaking can be predicted a day ahead without running a weather forecast. The fourth is a live model you can play with: a jet on a rotating planet that you can stir and disturb with the mouse.

  • 1 Blocking composite viewer

    Averages over many past blocking events (and over moving highs) at their onset, peak and decay, from the ERA5 reanalysis (a reconstruction of past weather). Fields include the terms that change potential vorticity (PV, a measure of the rotation and stability of the air), vertical motion and heating by condensation and radiation, split by how the upper-level waves break (anticyclonic or cyclonic wave breaking). A decomposition into intensification, movement and deformation patterns is also available.

  • 2 Northern Hemisphere forecast viewer

    Latest GFS, ECMWF AIFS-single, ECMWF IFS or Canadian GDPS forecast, 0–240 h (IFS runs from 06 and 18 UTC: 0–144 h), of wind (u, v), temperature (T), geopotential height (Z), vertical motion (ω) and humidity (q) at 850, 500 and 250 hPa (about 1.5, 5.5 and 10 km). Rectangular (0–90°N) or polar-stereographic view; one shaded and one contoured variable at a time; the upper-level lows and highs at 500 hPa with their tracks over the past five days. Click a centre for how far it has moved west–east and south–north.

  • 3 Air-parcel tracks in blocking events

    Launch air parcels anywhere in five blocks (Russia 2010, the North Atlantic 2019, the Pacific Northwest heat dome 2021, the California wildfires of January 2025, the Greenland high of January 2026) at about 380, 285 or 200 hPa, and watch a neural network continue their tracks 24 h forward and backward as a set of possible paths, next to their true paths from the ERA5 reanalysis. The network runs in your browser; it never sees a wind field, only how nearby parcels moved during the previous or next 48 h.

  • 4 Interactive β-plane model

    A two-dimensional model of the upper-level flow on a doubly periodic β-plane (the Coriolis parameter increasing northward), running live in your browser. Switch on a jet (centre latitude, width, strength) and random stirring in a storm-track band (centre latitude, zonal wavenumber, strength); drag the mouse to push the flow or click to drop highs and lows, and watch Rossby waves, wave breaking and stalled highs develop. Side panels show the zonal-mean wind, a Hovmöller diagram and the energy spectrum.
