Purpose. A block is a large high-pressure system in the middle and upper troposphere that stays in place for days to weeks and diverts the usual west-to-east procession of weather around itself. This page follows the air that moves through five blocks at about 380, 285 and 200 hPa (roughly 7–12 km above sea level). Choose an event, a time in its life cycle and a start level, then click on the map: a neural network continues the paths of the 16 air parcels nearest that point for 24 h into the future (forecast) and 24 h into the past (backcast), as a set of possible paths, and draws their true paths for comparison.

Terms. An air parcel is a small volume of air followed as it moves with the wind, in three dimensions; its track is the sequence of its positions. The true tracks are computed by the trajectory model LAGRANTO from the hourly winds of ERA5, the European reanalysis (a reconstruction of past weather from observations and a weather model). The network is a conditional Kinematic Interpolant (Jiang, Marzouk, Bose & Pooladian, 2026): it is never given a wind field; instead it predicts how a parcel accelerates from how 192 other parcels of the same group moved during the 48 h before (for a forecast) or after (for a backcast) the chosen time. Adding small random kicks to the predicted motion gives an ensemble of 16 possible tracks per parcel; their scatter, the spread, shows how uncertain the model is. Pressure is given in hectopascals (hPa); lower pressure means higher altitude.

How it works

Parcels for every place and time. The network needs observed tracks around the point, so each event comes with a dense set of true tracks: groups (patches) of 480 parcels filling a disk of 300 km radius, released every 12 h from four days before the event to its end, on centres about 1000 km apart over the event region and up to 40° of longitude upstream (west), at three start levels: 0.38, 0.285 and 0.2 of the surface pressure (about 380, 285 and 200 hPa where the surface pressure is 1000 hPa). Each patch is followed for 156 h. In total 4,050 patches, 1.9 million parcels.

A launch. For the forecast, among the patches of the chosen level released 48–132 h before the chosen time, the one with the most parcels within 300 km of the point is used; its 16 parcels nearest the point are launched, and 192 of its other parcels, observed every 3 h over the 48 h before the chosen time, are what the network sees. The backcast does the same with a patch released 24–108 h before the chosen time, observed over the 48 h after it, so the two directions may use different parcels near the point. The network then integrates position and velocity in steps of 10 minutes.

Accuracy. The network was trained on 25.3 million true tracks from ERA5 releases every 5 days of 2015–2017 at 20 levels from the surface to about 100 hPa. In 2021, a year it never saw, its deterministic 24-h forecast misses the true position by a median of 335 km and its backcast by 254 km, against 523 and 535 km for parcels moving straight on at their starting velocity. The size of the random kicks was chosen on the 2015–2017 validation parcels so that the 24-h spread matches the error; there the mean of 16-member ensembles misses by a median of 275 km at 24 h, against 338 km for the forecast without kicks. Blocks and their surroundings are not the average case, so errors near a block may differ.

Speed. The network runs in your browser (onnxruntime-web, on one processor core), stored with 8-bit weights, which changes its 24-h errors by less than their scatter from one set of random kicks to another. The first launch of a visit downloads it (40 MB); nothing is stored on your computer, and it is discarded when you leave the page. A launch then takes about 29 s on a typical computer. The tracks appear hour by hour while it runs.

Data, code and references

  • ERA5 hourly winds on 37 pressure levels at 0.25° (Copernicus Climate Change Service), read from Google’s public Analysis-Ready, Cloud-Optimized ERA5 store.
  • LAGRANTO trajectory model: Sprenger, M. and Wernli, H., The LAGRANTO Lagrangian analysis tool – version 2.0, Geosci. Model Dev., 8, 2569–2586, 2015, doi:10.5194/gmd-8-2569-2015.
  • Kinematic Interpolants: Jiang, Marzouk, Bose & Pooladian, Learning smooth dynamics via Kinematic Interpolants, arXiv:2610.22519 (2026).
  • Tracks and maps: Hugging Face dataset; the network: Hugging Face model; code: github.com/yanxingjianken/ki-lagrangian-tracks.
